HighLoan interest calculations#1

Loans on the same setting are repaid using different interest calculations

Who should look at this: Engineering (loan calculation) and Product

Status: Fix expected: the team expects the new loan integration to resolve this (23 November). It stays open until a re-run after that integration is live shows every loan following the same rule.

Customers on the same loan product are charged interest in different ways, and older loans may be charged the wrong amount. This affects money and must be confirmed before loans go live.

What we saw
Both loans are Personal loans at 8.5% set to “Monthly equal”. The newer loan #188 (₦500,000 over 9 months) collects ₦98,056 a month — the specification’s amount at 8.5% a month (₦98,055.56), rounded up to the whole naira. Every loan should follow the same rule from the loan specification. For loan #169 that is ₦633,333.33 a month if 8.5% is a monthly rate, or ₦243,750.00 if it is a yearly rate (loan #188: ₦98,055.56 or ₦59,097.23). Rounding up to the whole naira also means loan #188 would collect ₦4 more than the schedule over 9 months unless the last payment is adjusted.

HighLoan products (checked by hand)#3

Nano and Payday interest is not worked out by the number of days

Who should look at this: Engineering (loans calculation)

A 7-day loan costs as much as a 30-day loan, against the loan specification; customers repaying early are overcharged.

What we saw
23 November, loan calculator (POST /api/v3/loan-calculations, “Daily direct”): ₦20,000 at 0.3 costs ₦60 for 7 days and ₦60 for 30 days; the rate is charged once for the whole loan. The loan specification says interest = principal × daily rate × days (₦20,000 for 7 days at 0.30% a day = ₦420; for 30 days = ₦1,800). The portal field is “Flat interest rate (30-day term)”. Nano and Payday were set to 9% per 30-day term (= 0.30% a day) and left at a 30-day tenor only, so no one is overcharged for now.
What should happen
Charge daily-rate loans for the days chosen (or used, for early settlement), then offer the 1–30 day (Nano) and 7–30 day (Payday) tenors from the specification.

Remove before production

Test-only shortcuts the team confirmed are deliberate on the dev system. They are not counted as problems here, but each must be gone before go-live.

  • “Mark as paid” on loans (PATCH /api/v3/loan-applications/{id}/mark-as-paid). A temporary workaround to make testing easier; the team will remove it before production (confirmed 23 November). On dev it closes any loan as “REPAID”, including one that was never paid out: loan #95 (approved, never disbursed) was closed this way on 23 November. Before go-live, check the endpoint and any portal button for it are gone, and that approved-but-unpaid loans have a proper cancel action instead.

Differences from the documents (not failures)

These work, but don’t match what the specifications say. Product should confirm which is correct.

  • RWIN holding period. When booking an RWIN plan for a customer, the form asks them to accept a 1-day minimum holding period. The RWIN 2.0 specification says 30 days, with no exceptions.
  • Early-repayment charge on loans. Personal and Public Sector loan products have a “Liquidation charge” (3% by default). The loan specification says customers can repay early without any penalty.
  • Personal loan limits. A new Personal loan product starts at ₦50,000–₦6,000,000 and 3–18 months. The specification allows up to ₦30,000,000 and 3–24 months. (These can be changed by an admin.)
  • Daily-interest loans. Nano and Payday loans can be switched to a monthly interest model in the admin portal, although the specification says they are always charged per day.

Questions for the product team

  • What status should a written-off or force-closed loan have? The API’s loan statuses have no “Written off” or “Force closed”, while the admin requirements expect a separate “Written off” state. No loan on the test system has been written off or force-closed yet.
  • Should defaulted loans be written off? “Write off” only appears on overdue loans; a defaulted loan (for example #183) shows no actions at all.
  • Is the loan “Interest rate” shown in the portal a monthly rate (as in the specification’s 2% examples) or a yearly rate? Newer loans now treat it as monthly; loan #169 matches neither.
  • Should monthly loan repayments be rounded to the whole naira, and if so, is the difference corrected in the last payment?
  • Should existing loans (such as #169) be recalculated under the new interest rule, or keep their original schedule?
  • What is the minimum customer verification level (KYC tier) for a Personal loan? The specification does not say; the tests assume Tier 2.
  • Public Sector corridors: is the customer’s rate the “policy rate” or the “booked average interim rate”, and in what unit? The Lagos Civil Servants corridor stores 0.03 and 4; lagos-v1 stores 25 and 2.5. The app quotes 4% a month.
  • Public Sector fees: should the corridor’s 1% management fee and 1% quarterly fee (capitalised) apply, or the product’s “Public sector fee” structure (0%, deducted upfront, 7.5% VAT)? The app shows the first; the saved loans follow the second.

MediumAPI#7

Saved beneficiaries accept a bank that doesn’t exist, and can’t be edited

Who should look at this: Engineering (backend)

Customers can save recipients that can never be paid (a bank that doesn’t exist), and must delete and re-add a recipient to correct a typo.

What we saw
Signed in as a test customer: POST /api/v3/user-account-beneficiaries saved a recipient with bank code “999” and bank name “Not A Bank” (201; still so on 19 November). Currency is fixed: since 19 November anything other than NGN is refused (“Currency can only be NGN”). There is no endpoint to change a saved recipient: PUT and PATCH on /api/v3/user-account-beneficiaries/{id} answer 405. What works: a recipient with a real-style name and a 10-digit account number saves; “abc” or a 5-digit number is refused; the same recipient can’t be saved twice; another customer can’t see, search or delete it; delete works, and deleting again gives 404. The test recipients were deleted afterwards.
What should happen
Only accept bank codes from the supported bank list (ideally with an account-name check), and offer an update endpoint as announced.
MediumAPI#8

Credit reports fail with a server error for some loans

Approvers looking at these applications get a crash page instead of the bureau reports or a clear message.

The automatic check passed in the morning (it used #219) and failed in the afternoon (it used #221).

Return the reports, or a 4xx with a clear message when there are none for the loan.
LowAPI#9

Some endpoints refuse with the wrong error code

Who should look at this: Engineering (backend)

The request is refused, but with “bad request” (400) instead of “not signed in” (401) or “not allowed” (403), so apps can’t tell the customer to sign in again.

What we saw
2 endpoints: GET /api/v3/users/attestation; GET /api/v3/users/pep-declaration.

Refuse unsigned requests with 401 and wrong-role requests with 403.

The evidence for this problem is kept in the internal report.

LowAPI#10

Wallet history puts credits in the “debit” field

Who should look at this: Engineering (backend, wallet)

Anything that adds up credits and debits from the API (reports, exports, reconciliation) will get wrong totals.

What we saw
24 November, GET /api/v3/wallet-histories (customer): a ₦1,000 incoming bank transfer is returned as transactionType “CREDIT” with credit: null and debit: 1000; every entry, credit or debit, has its amount in “debit”. The app shows the direction correctly because it reads transactionType. The incoming transfer also has wemaInflow: false and no sender name (“Simulated inflow”), and on the receiving side of a wallet-to-wallet transfer the description names the receiver (“Transfer to Rosabon/<receiver>”) instead of the sender.
What should happen
Put the amount in “credit” for credits, mark Wema inflows, and describe a received transfer with the sender’s name.

Notes & questions

Gaps in the API document

The API works here, but its document is missing or contradicts something. Developers building against it will trip over these.

  • Pending address verification documented as a live stream. GET /api/v3/users/pending-address-verification is documented as returning text/event-stream (a live stream), but it returns a normal JSON page; a stream request is refused with 406 (24 November).
  • Paging starts at 1. List endpoints take pageNo starting at 1 — pageNo=0 is rejected as “pageNo is required”. Leaving out the optional pageNo on GET /api/v3/loan-products fails with “Page index must not be less than zero”. The API document says neither.
  • Parameters documented as optional but required. GET /api/v3/investment-products/{slug}/rate (Either tenorDays or tenorMonths is required).
  • Sign-in endpoints documented as needing a sign-in token. POST /api/v3/admin-users/login and POST /api/v3/users/login — tools generated from the document will try to send a token that doesn't exist yet.
  • Internal sign-in fields shown as request parameters. 162 endpoints list the server’s own sign-in fields (principal, credentials, authorities…) as parameters a caller could send. They should be hidden from the document.
  • Required fields are not marked. 100 of 189 request bodies don’t say which of their fields are mandatory, so callers only find out from errors. For example, the customer sign-in (POST /api/v3/users/login) lists email and password with neither marked required. Each endpoint below needs its mandatory fields listed in the document’s “required”.
