High priority

1. Loans on the same setting are repaid using different interest calculations

Area: Loan interest calculations · Who should look at this: Engineering (loan calculation) and Product

Customers on the same loan product are charged interest in different ways, and older loans may be charged the wrong amount. This affects money and must be confirmed before loans go live.

What we saw
Both loans are Personal loans at 8.5% set to “Monthly equal”. The newer loan #188 (₦500,000 over 9 months) collects ₦98,056 a month — the specification’s amount at 8.5% a month (₦98,055.56), rounded up to the whole naira. The older loan #169 (₦5,000,000 over 24 months) still collects ₦226,041.66 a month, which is what you get if 8.5% were the interest for the whole 24 months.
What should happen
Every loan should follow the same rule from the loan specification. For loan #169 that is ₦633,333.33 a month if 8.5% is a monthly rate, or ₦243,750.00 if it is a yearly rate (loan #188: ₦98,055.56 or ₦59,097.23). Rounding up to the whole naira also means loan #188 would collect ₦4 more than the schedule over 9 months unless the last payment is adjusted.

2. 7.5% “Loan VAT” is taken from every loan payout without being shown on the loan

Area: Loan interest calculations · Who should look at this: Engineering (loans) and Finance

All three loans show a management fee of ₦0, yet exactly 7.5% was kept back: #169 ₦5,000,000 → ₦4,625,000 paid out; #188 ₦500,000 → ₦462,500; #189 (paid out during this round) ₦100,000 → ₦92,500. The customer’s wallet history shows the full amount credited, then the 7.5% taken back as “Loan VAT”.

The amount paid out should equal the loan amount minus the charges shown on the loan. Finance should confirm whether VAT applies to the amount lent (it normally applies to fees); if it does, it should be shown on the offer and the loan record.

3. Force close, write-off and penalty waivers skip the approval workflow

Area: Loan Management (checked by hand) · Who should look at this: Engineering (admin portal and loans) and Compliance

5. Public Sector corridor rates are stored and shown in different units

Area: Product Management: corridors (checked by hand) · Who should look at this: Product and Engineering (loans)

Staff cannot tell from the portal what rate customers will be charged, and a rate typed in the wrong unit would go live unnoticed.

What we saw
Corridors & partners shows the Lagos Civil Servants rate as “0.03%” (stored as 0.03, with a booked average interim rate of 4), and lagos-v1 as “25%” (booked average 2.5). Its management fee (0.01) and quarterly fee (0.01) are stored as fractions; lagos-v1’s management fee is 2.5. The app quotes Lagos Civil Servants loans at 4% a month.
What should happen
One unit for every rate and fee, labelled in the portal (for example “% per month”), with the customer’s rate clearly identified.

Differences from the documents (not failures)

These work, but don’t match what the specifications say. Product should confirm which is correct.

  • RWIN holding period. When booking an RWIN plan for a customer, the form asks them to accept a 1-day minimum holding period. The RWIN 2.0 specification says 30 days, with no exceptions.
  • Early-repayment charge on loans. Personal and Public Sector loan products have a “Liquidation charge” (3% by default). The loan specification says customers can repay early without any penalty.
  • Personal loan limits. A new Personal loan product starts at ₦50,000–₦6,000,000 and 3–18 months. The specification allows up to ₦30,000,000 and 3–24 months. (These can be changed by an admin.)
  • Daily-interest loans. Nano and Payday loans can be switched to a monthly interest model in the admin portal, although the specification says they are always charged per day.

Questions for the product team

  • What status should a written-off or force-closed loan have? The API’s loan statuses have no “Written off” or “Force closed”, while the admin requirements expect a separate “Written off” state. No loan on the test system has been written off or force-closed yet.
  • Should defaulted loans be written off? “Write off” only appears on overdue loans; a defaulted loan (for example #183) shows no actions at all.
  • Is the loan “Interest rate” shown in the portal a monthly rate (as in the specification’s 2% examples) or a yearly rate? Newer loans now treat it as monthly; loan #169 matches neither.
  • Should monthly loan repayments be rounded to the whole naira, and if so, is the difference corrected in the last payment?
  • Should existing loans (such as #169) be recalculated under the new interest rule, or keep their original schedule?
  • What is the minimum customer verification level (KYC tier) for a Personal loan? The specification does not say; the tests assume Tier 2.
  • Public Sector corridors: is the customer’s rate the “policy rate” or the “booked average interim rate”, and in what unit? The Lagos Civil Servants corridor stores 0.03 and 4; lagos-v1 stores 25 and 2.5. The app quotes 4% a month.
  • Public Sector fees: should the corridor’s 1% management fee and 1% quarterly fee (capitalised) apply, or the product’s “Public sector fee” structure (0%, deducted upfront, 7.5% VAT)? The app shows the first; the saved loans follow the second.

High priority

1. Data is available without signing in

Area: API · Who should look at this: Engineering (backend) and Security

What we saw
1 endpoint answered with data to a request with no sign-in. The endpoint list is in the internal report — kept off this public page on purpose.

Every endpoint holding customer or business data should refuse requests that are not signed in (401).

Gaps in the API document

The API works here, but its document is missing or contradicts something. Developers building against it will trip over these.

  • Paging starts at 1. List endpoints take pageNo starting at 1 — pageNo=0 is rejected as “pageNo is required”. Leaving out the optional pageNo on GET /api/v3/loan-products fails with “Page index must not be less than zero”. The API document says neither.
  • Parameters documented as optional but required. GET /api/v3/investment-products/{slug}/rate (Either tenorDays or tenorMonths is required).
  • Public lists documented as needing sign-in. GET /api/v3/country; GET /api/v3/state/{countryId} answer without sign-in (reasonable for reference lists), but the document says sign-in is required.
  • Sign-in endpoints documented as needing a sign-in token. POST /api/v3/admin-users/login and POST /api/v3/users/login — tools generated from the document will try to send a token that doesn't exist yet.
  • Internal sign-in fields shown as request parameters. 131 endpoints list the server’s own sign-in fields (principal, credentials, authorities…) as parameters a caller could send. They should be hidden from the document.
  • Required fields are not marked. 85 of 161 request bodies don’t say which of their fields are mandatory, so callers only find out from errors. For example, the customer sign-in (POST /api/v3/users/login) lists email and password with neither marked required. Each endpoint below needs its mandatory fields listed in the document’s “required”.
